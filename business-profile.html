The practice

South African counsel, from an office in Centurion, for the matters that hold a commercial and a family life together.

Mathibele Mahlaela Attorneys is a law firm in Centurion, Pretoria. We advise businesses, families and individuals. The published work covers commercial and company law, corporate law, employment and labour, the resolution of disputes arising from contracts, public administration and procurement, property, consumer matters, wills and trusts, the administration of deceased estates, Road Accident Fund claims, and general legal advice.

The claim we make for the practice is a plain one, and it is the line we use of ourselves: tailored legal solutions, and counsel a client can trust. The work is arranged around the client’s question. The measure of a file is the outcome that can properly be achieved, explained in language the client can use.

The distinguishing fact of the practice is the road by which the principal came to it. Before private practice, Mathibele Mahlaela spent long service inside institutions that live by contract and by statute: Eskom, Transnet Pipelines and the South African Bureau of Standards, together with a period as legal manager at JPC. The daily work in those appointments is the work clients now bring to Centurion. Drafting, reviewing, vetting and negotiating agreements, including procurement and information-technology contracts. Advising when legislation changes. Managing litigation. Dealing with requests under the Promotion of Access to Information Act. The practice, recorded from November 2022, places that experience at the instruction of the client in front of us.

04 · The standard of the work Mathibele Mahlaela Attorneys

04

The standard of the work

Integrity, honesty and loyalty, and a commitment to the client that survives the first meeting.

Those words are the firm’s own, from the account we publish of ourselves. A profile is of little use if it leaves them as a motto. This is what they govern in the file.

Integrity

Advice that can be relied on includes the advice not to proceed. Where the law, the contract or the facts do not support the result a client hopes for, that is the advice we give. A result described as a win is a result the law will sustain.

Honesty

Prospects, time and cost are discussed early, in ordinary language. Where a step will change the cost or the risk, we say so before the step is taken. We do not publish a tariff in this profile, because a serious fee is agreed against a defined instruction.

Loyalty

A mandate is accepted only when we can give it our undivided professional attention. We consider conflicts before we accept work, and we will not act on both sides of the same dispute. The client’s information stays with the matter.

Satisfaction

A file is finished, not merely opened. Reporting is clear. Original documents are accounted for. A client should never have to guess where a matter stands, or whether the person on the other side of the desk still has it in hand.

05 · How an instruction proceeds Mathibele Mahlaela Attorneys

05

How an instruction proceeds

The sequence below is the order in which a matter is taken up. It is a professional method. It is not a service-level promise with invented response times. Urgent matters, including those with a statutory time limit, are identified at the first contact and are ordered accordingly.

  1. The question is identified. A consultation, in the office or by arrangement, establishes what has happened, what the client needs, and what the deadline is. Road Accident Fund claims, reviews of administrative action, and notices under a contract are typical of work in which the date matters as much as the merits.
  2. A conflict is considered before a mandate. We decline work we cannot do cleanly. If the right practitioner is a conveyancer, or counsel is required for a particular forum, that is said at this point.
  3. Scope and fees are agreed. The client receives a clear statement of what will be done, what we need from the client, and the basis of the fee. The basis may be a quoted amount for a defined piece of work, time spent where the scope cannot yet be fixed, or a tariff where one applies.
  4. The work is done by the person with conduct. Drafting, an opinion, a negotiation, representation at a forum, or the administration of an estate. The client is told when something material changes: a new document, a shift in the other side’s position, a date from the Master or from a tribunal.
  5. The advice is given in a form the client can use. A contract the client can sign, a letter the client can send, an opinion the client can act on, or a recommendation to stop. Technical language is translated. It is not used as a screen.
  6. The matter is closed properly. Signed originals are dealt with, estate accounts are taken through to the Master’s requirements, a settlement is recorded so that it can be enforced, and the client knows the file is at an end.
06 · The work Mathibele Mahlaela Attorneys

06

The work

Ten areas, stated as we publish them, with the kind of instruction each one actually is.

We do not print a list of named matters. Client work is confidential, and a profile that invents a deal sheet is of no use to anyone. What follows is the scope of each area, in the terms we use publicly, and the instructions a client may properly bring.

6.1

Commercial and company law

The contract is the centre of the practice. We draft and we review. We negotiate. When a contract has failed, we litigate and we arbitrate.

This is the work the principal did, for years, from inside large institutions: commercial and non-commercial agreements, procurement contracts, and information-technology contracts, taken from a first draft through vetting and negotiation. A private company instructing this office is instructing someone who has sat on the other side of that table.

An instruction may be

  • the drafting of a commercial agreement;
  • a review of the other side’s draft before it is signed;
  • a negotiation on price, duration, liability, termination and the documents that sit behind the signature;
  • advice when performance has broken down;
  • representation in litigation or arbitration arising from the contract.
6.2

Corporate law

We advise on regulatory compliance, on due diligence, and on mergers and acquisitions. For a practice of this size the work is transaction support and corporate advice for owner-managed and private companies, carried by the attorney with conduct. The scope of a transaction is settled before it runs, including whether a particular step needs another specialist.

An instruction may be

  • a due-diligence review before a purchase or an investment;
  • advice on a company’s obligations under the legislation that governs it;
  • support through a sale, a purchase or a restructuring of a private company;
  • the corporate documents that have to be right before a commercial contract is worth signing.
6.3

Employment and labour law

We act for employers and for employees. The work includes legal representation and the drafting of employment contracts. Because we are prepared to act on either side of the employment relationship, the conflict enquiry at the start of a matter is not a formality.

An instruction may be

  • an employment contract, or a suite of them, drafted for the way the business actually works;
  • advice to an employer before a decision is taken;
  • advice to an employee who has received a decision and needs to know what it means;
  • representation in a dispute arising from the employment relationship.
6.4

Alternative dispute resolution

We represent clients in disputes that come out of contracts, including construction contracts, at forums across South Africa. Where the contract has already chosen arbitration or another process, we advise on that clause and we appear in the process it creates. The aim, where the facts allow it, is a resolution that costs less than a trial and still protects the right.

An instruction may be

  • advice on a dispute-resolution clause before a contract is signed;
  • representation in a construction or other commercial dispute;
  • the conduct of an arbitration, or of a preliminary process the contract requires;
  • a frank view on whether the dispute should be settled, referred, or fought.
6.5

Public administration and procurement

The firm’s published specialisms include private and public administration law, and procurement law. This is the field in which the principal’s institutional career is most directly relevant: procurement-related contracts, legislative compliance, legal opinions, requests under the Promotion of Access to Information Act, and the management of litigation that follows a public decision.

Instructions in this field sit against the statutes that govern public contracting and administrative action. Among them are the Public Finance Management Act, the Municipal Finance Management Act, the Preferential Procurement Policy Framework Act, the Promotion of Administrative Justice Act and the Promotion of Access to Information Act. Naming those statutes describes the setting of the work. It is not a claim about any particular case.

An instruction may be

  • the drafting, vetting or negotiation of a procurement contract;
  • advice to a bidder, or to an institution, on the regularity of a procurement step;
  • a PAIA request, or the response to one;
  • an opinion on whether an administrative decision can stand;
  • management of the litigation that a public-law dispute becomes.
6.6

Property law

Property law is one of the areas the firm publishes. Advice on the rights and the documents is work an attorney undertakes. The transfer of land is reserved to a conveyancer. A client whose matter includes transfer should confirm, at the first consultation, how that part of the work will be attended to. We say so at the beginning, while the transaction can still be arranged properly.

An instruction may be

  • advice on a lease, a sale or another property contract before it is signed;
  • a dispute about occupation, payment or the meaning of a property agreement;
  • coordination, with a conveyancer where transfer is required, so that the legal advice and the transfer do not diverge.
6.7

Consumer law

Consumer law is part of the practice as the principal publishes it. The work is advice and representation where a transaction with a supplier, a credit provider or another business has gone wrong, and where the Consumer Protection Act or the contract between the parties governs the complaint.

An instruction may be

  • a reading of the contract and of the consumer’s actual rights under it;
  • correspondence that puts the complaint on a proper footing;
  • representation if the complaint has to be taken further.
6.8

Estate planning

We prepare wills and trust documents, so that a family’s assets pass in the way the client intends, and so that the people left behind are not left to guess. A will is a small document with a long consequence. It is drafted to be valid, to be findable, and to be capable of being administered.

An instruction may be

  • a will for a client whose affairs are straightforward, drafted so that they stay that way;
  • a will where there is a business, a blended family, or property that needs a specific direction;
  • a trust deed, where a trust is the right instrument and the client understands what it will demand of the trustees;
  • a review of an existing will that no longer matches the life it was written for.
6.9

Administration of deceased estates

We administer deceased estates, whether the deceased left a will or died intestate. The work is the reporting of the estate, dealing with the Master, the creditors and the heirs, and carrying the liquidation and distribution account through to its conclusion. It is done with care, because the people who bring this instruction are usually doing it once, and at a difficult time.

An instruction may be

  • the first report of a death to the Master, and the documents that report requires;
  • administration under a will;
  • administration where there is no will, and the Intestate Succession Act determines who inherits;
  • advice to an executor or an heir who is already inside an estate and needs to know whether it is being conducted properly.
6.10

Road Accident Fund claims, and general advice

We accept instructions on claims against the Road Accident Fund. Claims of this kind are governed by statute, and by time limits that can extinguish a right if they are missed. The practical protection is an early consultation, with the documents that exist: the accident report, the medical records, and any correspondence already received.

Alongside any single matter, we give general legal advice to businesses and individuals who do not keep a lawyer on the staff and who need one person to telephone when a contract, a letter or a regulatory question arrives. That retainer, where it is agreed, is the same standard as a once-off instruction. It is simply available before the problem has hardened.

Standing, and the limit of this profile

Attorneys in South Africa practise under the Legal Practice Act 28 of 2014. The Legal Practice Council, and its Provincial Councils, regulate legal practitioners and candidate legal practitioners, set norms and standards, and deal with admission, enrolment and professional conduct. A member of the public may search the Council’s practitioner register. This profile does not reproduce an enrolment number. A client or a counterparty who needs the number, or written confirmation of a Fidelity Fund certificate where trust money will be held, should ask the firm or consult the register. We treat that request as an ordinary one.

The Law Society of South Africa is a voluntary professional body. Its membership list records Mathibele Mahlaela, Pretoria, Mathibele Mahlaela Attorneys. Membership of that body is not a substitute for enrolment.

Nothing in this profile is legal advice on a reader’s own facts. Nothing in it is a warranty of a result. Public reviews describe past clients; they do not predict the next file. Company-register particulars were taken from a public compilation and should be confirmed against a current CIPC disclosure for any formal use. Where this document and a later certificate differ, the later certificate governs.

Client matters are confidential. The absence of a named deal list is deliberate.
